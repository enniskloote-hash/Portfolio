The Challenge

The work at Amway wasn't limited to a single product. As design lead within the connected products accelerator group, the role spanned four active product teams, each running in agile cycles with their own roadmaps, engineering constraints, and delivery timelines. Sky, the first connected product launched on a new AWS platform, anchored the work. But the broader challenge was building the infrastructure for how design operated across a growing connected product portfolio.

Coordinating across four teams meant managing competing priorities without direct authority over any of them. Each team operated independently but depended on shared services (authentication, data infrastructure, design standards) that the accelerator group was responsible for defining. Regional market constraints, shifting program definitions, and the need to align leadership, product, and delivery teams that didn't always share the same view of what the work required added continuous pressure to every sprint cycle.

Process

How We Approached It

The accelerator model required building operating infrastructure alongside the product itself: design work that would make four teams more effective, not just one. Work ran in sprints with quarterly planning across the portfolio, with Sky serving as the platform proving ground for patterns that other products would inherit.

Standards & Platform Foundation

Developed an accelerator playbook and design standards to create consistency across connected product experiences. Defined engagement models that clarified how the accelerator group collaborated with each product team, setting expectations for when and how design participated in sprint cycles.

Governance & Organizational Alignment

Facilitated decision-making between leadership, product, and delivery teams across the portfolio. Created visibility practices between innovation pipeline initiatives and active delivery teams to surface conflicts between roadmap aspirations and sprint capacity before they became blockers.

Delivery & Quality Practices

Introduced content development workflows that reduced rework by surfacing alignment gaps earlier in the process. Led usability testing efforts that educated teams on QA practices and evaluation standards. Coordinated post-launch planning across product, support, deployment, and security stakeholders.

Market Integration & Discovery

Defined integration approaches for the China launch and future regional expansion. Facilitated research and discovery for next-generation capabilities while helping teams adapt to shifting program definitions and evolving requirements without losing delivery momentum.

When one product team pushed for a distinctive mobile menu treatment, holding the shared pattern was the right call, not to limit creativity, but because those underlying UI elements were shared across the entire house of branded apps. Deviating there would have added development overhead that rippled across every product in the portfolio, not just one. The unique direction wasn't central to the product experience, and protecting cross-portfolio velocity outweighed the differentiation. Making that case with the team, and reaching alignment on it, was part of what operating as an accelerator required.

Solution

What We Delivered

Sky launched in China in 2017 and went through multiple release cycles, including extensions that connected the digital experience to physical product controls. The more durable outcome was the operating model the accelerator work established: design standards, engagement practices, and governance structures that subsequent connected products could build from rather than reinvent from scratch.
