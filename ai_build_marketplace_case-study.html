A Marketplace Built for Crisis

Designing an end-to-end donation-matching marketplace for a disaster-relief network — where the users are nonprofits operating under pressure, and every interaction has to feel effortless and credible.

The Challenge

The platform serves as a disaster-relief network — a place where nonprofits coordinate in-kind donations under pressure. These are users who have no margin for confusion. When they're in the product, something has gone wrong in the world, and they need to move fast. Low trust or high friction in the interface isn't a UX inconvenience — it's a mission failure.

The product had to earn credibility on contact. The brand system and the UX had to be designed together — every interface decision made with the full system in view, so the matching workflows could be complex under the hood without feeling that way to users under stress. The additional constraint: building in a way that lets the team extend the product independently, without me in every decision.

Process

How We Approached It

The work started in research — understanding how nonprofits actually coordinate donations in crisis conditions, where the pressure points are, and what "trust" looks like to a user who's operating under stress. From there, the design and brand work ran in parallel, so that every interface decision was made with the full system in view.

Solution

What We Delivered

A shipped marketplace where the brand system is built into the product from the start. The visual and interaction language are the same thing — so credibility is structural, not cosmetic. Design standards were codified so the team can extend the product on-brand, without needing a designer in the room for every decision.
